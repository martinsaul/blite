Privacy Policy

Bellion Share is a screen-sharing app for small groups of friends, for Windows and Linux, with an Android app for watching. This policy explains what data they handle. Last updated: November 27, 2026.

What we collect and why

  • Display name and tag. You choose a name; the app adds a random tag (like #1234). Others in your session see it.
  • Session data. To connect people, our server briefly holds who is in which session, along with their network address (IP address and port). Network addresses stay in memory only. So that a server restart doesn't end calls, the server also saves its open sessions to its own disk every 30 seconds: each session's room code, a hash of its password, the voice channel it's tied to (if any), its owner and banned members, and for each member their display name, public identity key, app version and a hash of the token that lets them reconnect. The file only holds sessions that are open: a session leaves it at the next save after it ends (5 minutes after the last person leaves).
  • Your screen and audio. These go directly between the people in a session (peer to peer), or through a member who volunteers as a relay. Our server runs no TURN server, so nothing passes through it. Sessions are encrypted end to end by default; a relay is a room member and can see what it forwards.
  • Discord (optional). If you press "Connect Discord", Discord asks you to let Bellion see which voice channel you are in. We use this only to show a session started in that channel, and to tie your session to it.
  • Discord voice channel. When you connect Discord in your browser, our server keeps your Discord user ID and name, tied to your app's identity key, until you disconnect. While the app is running, our Discord bot tells it which voice channel you are in, on servers the bot was added to. The bot follows only people whose Bellion Share app is running and connected: Discord sends it everyone's voice channel changes on those servers, and it drops all others at once, without storing or logging them. Your own channel is kept in memory only, and forgotten when the app closes.
  • Discord login. Our server passes the one-time login code to Discord and hands the access token back to the app, without keeping it. The app keeps the token on your computer only. The server keeps the ID of a voice channel tied to a live session until the session ends (in memory, and in the saved sessions described above). We do not read your messages, servers, friends or anything else.
  • Logs and crash reports. The app writes a log on your computer. If the app crashes or exits uncleanly, on the next start it asks before sending the error and the part of that log just before it to our server (in Settings > Crash reports you can also set it to send automatically or never). When you press "Send logs" or "Send feedback", it sends the log and your message. Crash reports and "Send logs" are anonymized by default: names, room codes, servers, window titles and your computer's and user names are replaced by placeholders, unless you untick "Anonymize" when sending logs (which then applies to later reports too, until you tick it again). Logs contain your operating system and hardware, your network addresses, the addresses of people you were in a session with, and (when not anonymized) your display name and theirs. We use them only to fix problems. They are deleted within 72 hours. The app shortens public network addresses to a code that keeps only the network part (for example 203.0.x.x), and removes room passwords from the log before writing it, and from everything it sends. Older versions on Windows wrote the whole share link, including the room password, to the local log when you opened one, so it could reach us in a crash report or when you sent logs.
  • Logs and crash reports on Android. The Android app keeps on your phone, for a week: a log of what it did (rooms joined, connections, video decoders, errors), measurements taken each second of a call (frames received, decoded and shown, freezes, lost packets, round-trip time, bitrate, the network type and signal strength, battery level, temperature, CPU and memory use), a description of the phone (model, Android version, screen, video decoders) and crash reports (what the app was doing when it crashed, froze or was closed by Android because of an error). Crash reports are sent to our server automatically the next time you open the app. Everything else is sent only when you tap "Send logs" in Settings, with your note if you write one. Reports include your display name. As on the desktop, we use them only to fix problems, delete them within 72 hours, and remove room passwords and keys before anything is written or sent. Video and sound are never recorded.
  • Camera (Android). The Android app asks for the camera only when you tap the button to scan a share link's QR code. It reads the QR code on your phone, as the camera sees it; it doesn't save pictures and doesn't send them anywhere. You can say no and paste the link instead.
  • Notifications (Android). While you're in a room, Android shows a notification so the room's sound keeps playing when the app is in the background. It is shown on your phone only.
  • Crash dumps (Windows). If the app crashes, it saves a crash dump on your computer. Dumps are never uploaded. They don't contain the app's full memory, and the app keeps at most two, for up to 14 days. Older versions wrote dumps with the app's full memory, which can include session keys. You can delete them from %LOCALAPPDATA%\Blite\crash.
  • Your IP address and your router. People in a session connect to each other directly, so they learn each other's IP address. To make that easier, the Windows and Linux app asks your router to open UDP port 30471 (UPnP, NAT-PMP or PCP) by default; start it with -udp-port 0 to turn that off. The Android app doesn't.
  • Room passwords. The password in a share link is in the part after #, which your browser doesn't send to us. The app sends it to our server over an encrypted connection to check it when you join; the server keeps only a hash of it, in memory and in its saved sessions, until the session ends.
  • Server logs. Our servers log requests, including IP addresses, for security and abuse prevention. These logs are kept only as long as needed for that.
  • Updates. The app checks our update server for new versions. That server sees your IP address and the app version. The Android app does this by fetching https://bellion.app/download/android.json at most every six hours while it is open; if you installed it from Google Play, updates come from Google Play.

What we don't do

We don't sell your data, show ads, or use trackers or analytics. We don't record sessions.

Sharing

We share data only with Discord, when you use "Connect Discord", and as required by law.

Your choices

You can disconnect Discord at any time in the app (Settings > Disconnect Discord), which deletes the link on our server. You can also remove Bellion in Discord: User Settings > Authorized Apps. What the app keeps on your computer (its settings, the Discord token and logs) is in its settings and log folders, which you can delete. On Android, clearing the app's storage or uninstalling it deletes its settings, identity key and logs from the phone. To have your reports deleted earlier, or for any question, contact us at admin@zenithnetwork.com.

Children

Bellion Share is not meant for children under 13.

Changes

If this policy changes, we update this page and the date above.
