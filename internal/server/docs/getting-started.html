Getting started

No account needed: install the app, pick what to share, press Go live, and send the link.

1. Install

  • Windows 10 or 11 (64-bit): download the installer and run BellionShare-Setup.exe. It installs the latest version for your user account (no admin rights needed) and adds Start menu and desktop shortcuts. The installer is code-signed (publisher: Martin Saul). The signature is new, so for a while Windows may still say "Windows protected your PC": choose More info, then Run anyway. Prefer no installer? Use the portable zip: unzip it anywhere and run blite-guard.exe.
  • Linux (Ubuntu 24.04, Mint 22 or newer, 64-bit): download the .deb, open a terminal in its folder and run sudo apt install ./blite_*.deb, then start Bellion Share from your app menu. Bellion Share needs an X11 session; Wayland sessions aren't supported yet.

Bellion Share keeps itself up to date: when a new version is out it offers it in the app, and installing restarts it (and rejoins your call).

Closing the window keeps Bellion Share running in the tray, so share links still open it. Use the tray icon to open the window again or quit. Start Bellion Share when I sign in, in the gear (Settings) menu at the top right, starts it in the tray at sign-in.

2. Pick your name

The top of the window shows who you are, for example You are Alice#4417. Press Change name... to set the name others see. The 4-digit tag after it comes from your identity key, a key the app creates on your computer, so two people can't pass as each other by picking the same name.

3. Share your screen

The Share your screen card on the home screen sets up what you share:

  1. Pick Entire screen (your main monitor, the one with the taskbar or panel) or A window, which lists your monitors and windows, with each app's icon.
  2. Pick a quality, for example 1080p, 60 fps (see Quality presets).
  3. Press Go live and copy link. Bellion Share starts a session, shares what you picked and copies the share link to your clipboard.
  4. Paste the link to your friends, for example in a Discord chat.

Sound. Picking a window shares that app's sound too, and the card shows With sound. Picking the entire screen doesn't turn sound on: the card shows No audio. Click it to open Share options and pick the sound you share (see Sound). If Bellion Share can't tell which app a window belongs to, it shares the window without sound and says so.

Share options also has an Advanced... section with more sources (all screens combined, a test pattern, or nothing, to only watch), a custom frame rate, and the session's security:

  • Require a password: the session gets a password you choose. Without one, the session is open to anyone who has the link. Either way the link carries everything needed to join, so only send it to people you want in.
  • Encrypt end to end (on by default): each sender encrypts its video and audio with keys that only people in the room get, so a TURN server passing them on can't read them. Everyone in the room needs build 72 or newer.

A password or turned-off encryption shows as a small tag on the card, so you see it before you go live. Both apply when you start a new session.

If you use Discord, you can also go live straight in your voice channel: see Discord. Until Discord approves the Bellion app, this works only for a few people added as testers.

4. Watch someone

Click the share link someone sent you. Your browser opens a page that hands the link to Bellion Share (on its first use it may ask whether to open the app). If nothing opens, make sure Bellion Share is installed and running, then click the link again.

You can also paste the link, or just the room code (like K7PQ-2M4X), into Watch someone and press Watch. If the session has a password, the app asks for it. When you switch to Bellion Share with a share link on your clipboard, it offers a one-click button to watch that session.

Recent rooms on the home screen lists the last 5 sessions you started or joined: click one to rejoin it. Sessions that have ended show Ended and fade away; the x removes one from the list.

You join watching only. To share your own screen too, press Go live: main monitor in the call: everyone in a room can share at the same time.

5. In the call

  • The top shows the room code and whether the call is end-to-end encrypted. Copy link copies the link again to invite more people; Leave ends your part in the call.
  • Go live: main monitor / Stop sharing starts or stops your share; Share a window... (or Share something else...) picks what you share, and Share options... changes the quality and sound.
  • People lists you and everyone else, with their connection and the quality you get from them. Click someone to open their panel: their volume (up to 200%), mute, their video quality and connection details. The eye next to each person who shares starts or stops watching them.
  • The safety code is the same for everyone when nobody is intercepting the connection. Click the End-to-end encrypted badge to see it, and read it aloud once to check.
  • The screens you watch open in a video window: see Watching.

You can rejoin with the link while the session is open; a session ends shortly after the last person leaves.
